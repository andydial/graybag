| M9 | **The ledger's sign convention is structural, not a convention** — `E06-31`, migration `0013`. Three things: a CHECK constraint pins `normal_balance` per `account_type` (wallet, payable, tax_payable, revenue → credit; receivable, provider_clearing, provider_fees, suspense → debit); `ledger_balance(account_id)` is the **only** way to read a balance and consults the account's own `normal_balance`; and `assert_ledger_integrity()` checks structural invariants nightly rather than recomputing a derived balance. Also seeds the eleven `category = 'ledger'` reason codes (`E06-22`), without which no posting could be written at all. | Andy's ruling 2026-08-10: resolve it structurally rather than by picking a convention and writing it down. **The failure being designed out is one that does not look like a bug.** A wallet is a liability and a provider clearing account is an asset, so their balances run in opposite directions. A single-signed `balance()` helper — "debits minus credits" — returns plausible numbers for both and correct ones for half, and the nightly assertion meant to catch the drift is the thing computing it wrongly. The numbers are numbers; nothing is red. Making the mapping a constraint means a wallet cannot be *created* debit-normal, and making the balance function consult the account means a caller cannot pick a sign at all — there is only one helper and it decides. The nightly check deliberately does **not** compare two derivations of the same quantity, because two derivations sharing one sign error agree with each other and the check passes in exactly the case it exists for. What it does compare is `wallet_balance` against the ledger (`I8`, `[DM-04]`), which earns its place because those two are maintained by different code paths. Zero-sum-per-transaction was already enforced by a deferred constraint trigger in `0001` and is untouched. Still missing: `bank` on `ledger_account_type` (`E06-23`), left out because `ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE` cannot be used in the transaction that adds it and the pairing belongs with the settlement work. |

| M10 | **Bonus meal-pack items carry no value in the ledger.** `meal_pack.items_original` is the deferred-revenue denominator for the life of the pack and never changes; bonus items live in a separate `bonus_remaining` column, are granted with **no ledger posting**, are spent with **no ledger posting**, and are forfeited at expiry with **no ledger posting**. Valued items are always spent before bonus items | Decided by Andy 2026-09-16, overruling my proposal. The alternative — raise the denominator to 22 on grant and re-spread the per-item value — is what "the per-item value drops accordingly" literally requires, and I recommended it because it keeps the invariant exactly true by construction. **The reason it was rejected is the timing**: the bonus is earned at the instant the last *original* item is served, so at that moment the pack's deferred balance is already zero and all ₹3,000 is recognised revenue. Re-spreading would therefore mean `Dr revenue / Cr deferred` — **a reversal of revenue already booked, possibly in a closed month**. A pack bought in March earning its bonus in April would reverse part of March's revenue in April. Andy's ruling avoids that entirely, at a cost worth stating: revenue per *served meal* is no longer constant, because 22 meals are served against ₹3,000 recognised over 20 — so margin on a bonus-earning pack is lower than the revenue line suggests, and revenue-per-order reads high for the two bonus orders. **The separate columns are the enforcement**: with no combined `items_total` there is no arithmetic anywhere that can accidentally mix a free item into a valued one. See `docs/meal-packs-rebuild.md` §5 |
| M11 | **The meal-pack deferred balance is a function, never an accumulated column.** `half_up(price_paid_paise × valued_remaining, items_original)`. Every ledger event — redeem, reverse, expire — posts the **difference of that function before and after**, and no amount is ever computed another way | Non-negotiable #3 has no exception for a proportion, and a stored per-item constant cannot survive it: ₹3,000 over 20 divides evenly but ₹5,000 over 40 will not for every price an admin can type, and an invariant that is *nearly* true is not one. Defining the balance as a pure function of `(price_paid_paise, valued_remaining, items_original)` makes the invariant exact in integer paise at every instant, and makes the last valued item of a pack land on exactly zero whatever the arithmetic on the way there. **The invariant is then checkable as one equality** — `balance(platform:deferred_revenue:meal_packs) = Σ half_up(price_paid_paise × valued_remaining, items_original)` over packs with `status in ('active','exhausted')` — which is false the instant an item is counted twice, lost, or recognised without being spent. It also holds *continuously* across a pack expiring before the sweep notices, because an unswept pack is still `active` with its balance intact; the sweep moves status, items and ledger in one transaction, so it is bookkeeping being timely rather than correctness being restored |
