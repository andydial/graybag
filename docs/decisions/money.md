| M9 | **The ledger's sign convention is structural, not a convention** — `E06-31`, migration `0013`. Three things: a CHECK constraint pins `normal_balance` per `account_type` (wallet, payable, tax_payable, revenue → credit; receivable, provider_clearing, provider_fees, suspense → debit); `ledger_balance(account_id)` is the **only** way to read a balance and consults the account's own `normal_balance`; and `assert_ledger_integrity()` checks structural invariants nightly rather than recomputing a derived balance. Also seeds the eleven `category = 'ledger'` reason codes (`E06-22`), without which no posting could be written at all. | Andy's ruling 2026-08-10: resolve it structurally rather than by picking a convention and writing it down. **The failure being designed out is one that does not look like a bug.** A wallet is a liability and a provider clearing account is an asset, so their balances run in opposite directions. A single-signed `balance()` helper — "debits minus credits" — returns plausible numbers for both and correct ones for half, and the nightly assertion meant to catch the drift is the thing computing it wrongly. The numbers are numbers; nothing is red. Making the mapping a constraint means a wallet cannot be *created* debit-normal, and making the balance function consult the account means a caller cannot pick a sign at all — there is only one helper and it decides. The nightly check deliberately does **not** compare two derivations of the same quantity, because two derivations sharing one sign error agree with each other and the check passes in exactly the case it exists for. What it does compare is `wallet_balance` against the ledger (`I8`, `[DM-04]`), which earns its place because those two are maintained by different code paths. Zero-sum-per-transaction was already enforced by a deferred constraint trigger in `0001` and is untouched. Still missing: `bank` on `ledger_account_type` (`E06-23`), left out because `ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE` cannot be used in the transaction that adds it and the pairing belongs with the settlement work. |

| M10 | **Pack money is never merged into the food revenue line, and every pack figure is derived from one function rather than accumulated.** `E21`, 2026-09-16. A pack sale is a **liability** on the day it happens — `Cr platform:deferred_revenue:meal_packs` — and becomes revenue only as items are spent. So `/admin/sales` gains a separate pack section and the existing revenue figures keep meaning *food sold*, unchanged. The six figures Andy asked for are: packs sold (count, gross ₹, GST ₹) by offer and period; deferred balance outstanding; revenue recognised from redemptions this period; breakage at expiry; redemption rate per offer; and bonus items granted and redeemed, shown **separately** from purchased items. Every one of them is computed from `half_up(price_paid_paise × items_remaining, items_total)` — the same expression the mobile thread's design posts every ledger movement as a *difference* of — and **never** from a stored per-item value. | Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters. **First**, a stored per-item value cannot be exact in integer paise: ₹3,000 over 22 items is 13,636.36 paise, so any accumulated balance drifts and an invariant that is *nearly* true is not one. Defining the balance as a function of `items_remaining` means the last item of a pack always lands on exactly zero, whatever happened on the way. **Second**, deriving the report from the same expression the ledger posts differences of means the report and the books **cannot disagree** — a reporting bug would have to be a ledger bug. The alternative, summing `order_line` or reading a cached column, produces a number that looks right and reconciles to nothing. **The denominator is `items_original`, never `items_total`** — Andy settled the bonus question on 2026-09-16: **bonus items carry no deferred value.** The parent paid ₹3,000 for 20 items and all of it is properly earned by the moment the bonus triggers; the bonus items are a **giveaway**, a cost to COGS when redeemed, and never a reversal of revenue already recognised. An earlier draft of this decision claimed the module was *policy-neutral* between that ruling and the restating alternative, on the grounds that both changed only `items_total`. **That was wrong and is struck**: under the actual ruling, dividing by `items_total` after a grant re-defers ₹272.73 of revenue correctly recognised in an earlier month — exactly the prior-period restatement Andy ruled out. There is no bonus restatement anywhere in the reporting path and there must never be one. A second consequence, recorded because it is a trap rather than a detail: `items_total` itself is **ambiguous**. Andy's wording is *"items_total stays 20 for value purposes"*; the mobile thread's schema carries `check (items_total = items_original + bonus_items)` once granted, making it 22. Money is safe either way because the denominator is `items_original`, but **item counts are not**, so `summarisePackSales` derives items-held from `items_original + granted bonus` rather than reading the column, and a test asserts the report is identical under both readings. The disagreement is raised as a requirement on the mobile thread rather than absorbed silently. |
| M11 | **Pack sales, GST and breakage are reported gross and never netted against each other.** `E21`, 2026-09-16. Breakage — items forfeited at expiry — is recognised as revenue (`Dr deferred / Cr revenue:breakage`) and is shown on its **own line**, not folded into "revenue recognised from redemptions". Likewise GST collected on a pack sale is shown separately from the ex-tax price and is **not** part of deferred revenue at all: the tax point is the sale (`0077`, accountant 2026-08-27), so the whole GST amount is a `tax_payable` liability on day one and never moves as items are spent. | Netting hides the two things the report exists to tell Andy. Revenue that arrived because a parent *ate* is a healthy pack; revenue that arrived because a parent *forgot* is a pricing or a communication problem, and a combined figure is identical in both cases — which is exactly the question "is this pack priced right" is asking. Keeping GST out of the deferred balance is not a presentation choice: deferred revenue is what we owe in **food**, and the tax was never owed in food. Mixing them would make the deferred balance disagree with `platform:deferred_revenue:meal_packs` by precisely the tax, every time. |
| M13 | **The back office reports pack money from `meal_pack_money`, and reports *blindness* as a first-class state.** `E21`, 2026-09-16. The view computes every per-pack figure in the database — `deferred_paise` via `meal_pack_deferred_paise`, `revenue_recognised_paise`, `breakage_paise`, `valued_items_redeemed`, `bonus_items_redeemed` — and `admin-pack-reports.ts` only sums and groups them. It does **not** replay redemptions in TypeScript, and it does **not** re-derive the deferred balance for comparison against the ledger; `check_meal_pack_ledger_invariant` already asserts that database-side and nightly. Separately, `packVisibilityOf` distinguishes *zero* from *cannot see*, and `/admin/sales` renders a warning instead of a number whenever the answer is the latter. | **On the arithmetic:** an earlier draft did replay redemptions to difference the deferred function, which would have been a second place for the money rules to live — the mistake `admin-reports.ts` warns about in its own header — and a *third* derivation of a number the ledger already holds exactly. Two copies agree until the day they do not, and the day they do not is the day somebody is reconciling a real month. **On blindness, which is the part that earns its own decision:** `meal_pack_money` is `security_invoker` over `meal_pack`, whose only read policy is `meal_pack_read_own`. A back-office account owns no packs, so the read **succeeds and returns nothing**, and naive aggregation prints a confident **₹0 still owed in food**. That is `E21-63`'s finding in a second place, and worse here — ₹0 owed is a sentence Andy would act on, and it is the difference between *we have no liability* and *we cannot measure our liability*. The ambiguity is resolvable rather than guessable: the sold **count** comes from the `admin-pack-offer` Edge Function under the service role, so packs-sold > 0 while the view returns none is a permission boundary, not an empty business. Every uncertain case resolves to blind, because a screen that says "we cannot see this" when a number was available is an annoyance, and one that says ₹0 when the truth is ₹40,000 is a false statement about money. |

| M12 | **A redeemed bonus item is a COGS line, not negative revenue.** `E21`, 2026-09-16, following Andy's ruling that bonus items carry no deferred value. Nothing was ever deferred against a bonus item, so redeeming one recognises no revenue and reverses none; its only money consequence is that we cooked a meal nobody paid for. `bonusCogsPaise` is reported on its own line and is **not** subtracted from `recognisedTotalPaise`, which is redemptions plus breakage and nothing else. | The tempting shape is to treat a giveaway as a discount and net it off revenue, which would make pack revenue read lower than the cash actually earned and would not reconcile to the ledger — the ledger has no such posting, because no such event happens on the revenue side. Keeping it as a cost also keeps the number **useful**: bonus COGS against redemption rate is what says whether the giveaway is buying repeat purchase or just giving food away, and a netted figure answers neither question. |

| M14 | **Bonus meal-pack items carry no value in the ledger.** `meal_pack.items_original` is the deferred-revenue denominator for the life of the pack and never changes; bonus items live in a separate `bonus_remaining` column, are granted with **no ledger posting**, are spent with **no ledger posting**, and are forfeited at expiry with **no ledger posting**. Valued items are always spent before bonus items | Decided by Andy 2026-09-16, overruling my proposal. The alternative — raise the denominator to 22 on grant and re-spread the per-item value — is what "the per-item value drops accordingly" literally requires, and I recommended it because it keeps the invariant exactly true by construction. **The reason it was rejected is the timing**: the bonus is earned at the instant the last *original* item is served, so at that moment the pack's deferred balance is already zero and all ₹3,000 is recognised revenue. Re-spreading would therefore mean `Dr revenue / Cr deferred` — **a reversal of revenue already booked, possibly in a closed month**. A pack bought in March earning its bonus in April would reverse part of March's revenue in April. Andy's ruling avoids that entirely, at a cost worth stating: revenue per *served meal* is no longer constant, because 22 meals are served against ₹3,000 recognised over 20 — so margin on a bonus-earning pack is lower than the revenue line suggests, and revenue-per-order reads high for the two bonus orders. **The separate columns are the enforcement**: with no combined `items_total` there is no arithmetic anywhere that can accidentally mix a free item into a valued one. See `docs/meal-packs-rebuild.md` §5 |
| M15 | **The meal-pack deferred balance is a function, never an accumulated column.** `half_up(price_paid_paise × valued_remaining, items_original)`. Every ledger event — redeem, reverse, expire — posts the **difference of that function before and after**, and no amount is ever computed another way | Non-negotiable #3 has no exception for a proportion, and a stored per-item constant cannot survive it: ₹3,000 over 20 divides evenly but ₹5,000 over 40 will not for every price an admin can type, and an invariant that is *nearly* true is not one. Defining the balance as a pure function of `(price_paid_paise, valued_remaining, items_original)` makes the invariant exact in integer paise at every instant, and makes the last valued item of a pack land on exactly zero whatever the arithmetic on the way there. **The invariant is then checkable as one equality** — `balance(platform:deferred_revenue:meal_packs) = Σ half_up(price_paid_paise × valued_remaining, items_original)` over packs with `status in ('active','exhausted')` — which is false the instant an item is counted twice, lost, or recognised without being spent. It also holds *continuously* across a pack expiring before the sweep notices, because an unswept pack is still `active` with its balance intact; the sweep moves status, items and ledger in one transaction, so it is bookkeeping being timely rather than correctness being restored |
