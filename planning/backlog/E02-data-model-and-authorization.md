## Why this is critical

In Bubble, `Order` is readable by everyone and 10 types have no privacy rules at all — including `Child` (minors' names, class, allergies, parent email). Authorization must be enforced server-side and covered by tests before any feature is built on top of it.

## Design decisions already locked

- Two planes: **Customer** (single ordering role) and back-office **grants** (KitchenOperator, SchoolViewer, PlatformAdmin), scoped to kitchen/school ids.
- School lives on the **Recipient**, not the user. Recipient = self or a dependent.
- `Menu` is owned by a Kitchen; `MenuAssignment(school, menu, valid_from, valid_to)` decides who sees what.
- Config **resolution chain**: platform default -> kitchen -> school. Applies to cutoff time, prices, break/drop times, revenue-share %.
- Effective config is **snapshotted onto the order** at write time (correctness + performance).
- **Ledger** (append-only credits/debits with reason codes) exists from v1 even without a visible wallet.

- [x] `E02-26` (risk:high) **Every function in `public` was executable by `anon`, because Postgres grants EXECUTE to PUBLIC by default and nothing had ever revoked it.** Found 2026-08-09 while writing `[AUTH-01]`'s "anon may execute exactly two functions" assertion, which failed with eleven. - [ ] `E02-39` (owner:andy) **One owner account, derived rather than enumerated.** Andy, 2026-08-28: *"Stop granting me permissions one at a time… my preference is that the owner is derived rather than enumerated: a single recorded account against which permission checks pass, not a list of grants somebody keeps in step."* Design in `docs/proposals/E02-39-derived-platform-owner.md`, **not yet built** — it needs a migration number the mobile thread holds, and a decision from Andy on one line in particular. **The shape**: a single-row `platform_owner` table and one boolean `or` inside `auth_has_permission`, which is the chokepoint every `auth_can*` already resolves through. Its own table rather than a `platform_config` key, because config is editable by anyone holding `config.platform_edit` and an owner stored there would be a permission that can grant itself everything. No write policy at all, so changing it takes a migration; `reason` is `not null`, so it cannot change without a stated why; an append-only history table makes the change visible, because difficulty is not visibility. **The line needing Andy's yes**: the short-circuit goes in `auth_has_permission` and *not* in `auth_can_reach_recipient` and its siblings. Those answer a relationship question — is there a live guardian link — not a permission question, and extending ownership into them would silently make one account the guardian of every child, reading every allergy note on a table designed so access follows a link somebody created. **Owner derives permissions, never relationships.** **The consequence easiest to miss**: the back office would render *empty* for the owner, because `nav-mount` reveals links from `permission_grant` rows and the owner has none — so it needs `auth_is_owner()` through to `visibleNav` and a `describeAccess` label of its own.
