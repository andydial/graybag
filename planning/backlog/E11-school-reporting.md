## Context

Decided against a school login portal — the PDF lands in the principal's inbox instead of waiting to be discovered.

## Tasks

- [ ] `E11-01` Monthly aggregate report per school: orders, revenue, school's share earned
- [ ] `E11-02` Historical month-on-month trend and cumulative total paid to date
- [ ] `E11-03` **Aggregates only** — no individual child names or allergies
- [ ] `E11-04` Designed on brand, presenting the partnership benefit (supporting underprivileged kids)
- [ ] `E11-05` Automated monthly email to the school contact, with the PDF attached
- [ ] `E11-06` Admin can preview, regenerate and manually re-send any month
- [ ] `E11-07` Reports computed from the **partitioned and indexed** tables of `E02-11`. Materialised aggregates stay deferred to `E18-22` until volume demands them
- [x] `E11-08` **A growth report — registrations over time and by school.** Andy: *"All registered users - registrations per date - registrations per school etc. Want to be able to see exactly how the registrations / growth is tracking. Would be good to have some basic graphs / visuals."* `/admin/growth`, requiring `users.view` at platform scope, deliberately separate from `/reports` — that one answers "what did this school order" for a school, this answers "is the product growing" for us, and `E10-10` exists to stop the monthly report quietly growing into something nobody agreed to. **Every number is a count and nobody is named**: `fetchGrowth` selects three columns per child (`id`, `school_id`, `created_at`) and the types have nowhere to put a name, so non-negotiable #4 is enforced at the boundary rather than in a template — `recipient_read_admin` *would* return every column. Five headline figures, a cumulative line with a per-day bar row on the same axis, and a per-school table. **A family is counted once per school**, however many children they have there: two siblings is one family, and counting two overstates reach by exactly the families most likely to be a reference. Days with no signups are plotted, because charting only the days that had one turns a flat fortnight into steady growth. Dates are **IST calendar dates** — bucketing a `timestamptz` by its UTC date files every signup after 18:30 IST under yesterday. Charts are hand-rolled inline SVG: the site ships zero third-party assets and the CSP is `script-src 'self'`, so a chart library is not an option, and a polyline is twenty lines. **"Signed up, no child yet" is on the headline row** because `AR7` makes signup-to-first-order a primary goal and an account with no child cannot order at all — that number is whether onboarding works. 3,230 B of JS against a 10,000 budget
- [ ] `E11-09` Growth by **month** as well as by day, once there is more than one month of data to compare. Deliberately not built now: with seventeen days of history a month view is one bar
- [x] `E11-10` (risk:high) **`/reports` was counting unpaid orders as revenue, and its date control did nothing.** Andy: *"The /reports is very bad and does not even show the right data. It shows orders and I updated date to at least include 1 more order from the new range and it didn't change any numbers. Also reports does not have option to do per school or per month etc."* Three faults. **(1) The money was wrong.** `summarise` did `if cancelled or refunded … else add to gross`, which swept `draft` and `pending_payment` into revenue — on production that was ₹228.92 of takings nobody had paid, and a number being too big is the one error on a report that never gets questioned. Statuses are now explicit sets: `paid`/`preparing`/`delivered` are earned, `draft`/`pending_payment` are counted and valued in their **own** column, `cancelled`/`refunded` counted and excluded, and **an unrecognised status throws** rather than being guessed onto a side of the ledger — guessing is exactly how this happened. **(2) The date control applied only on submit.** The query was always correct (extending to `2026-08-21` does return one more order, confirmed read-only against production); the form just showed the previous answer until you pressed Show. It now applies on change, and five presets mean the common ranges need no typing. **(3) No grouping.** Day, month and school are a control, and all three are folds of one set of (day, school) rows via `groupRows` — three queries would let the per-school total disagree with the per-month one, which is worse than no report. Verified by a test that every grouping folds to the same total. `REPORT_ORDER_COLUMNS` is unchanged: still no recipient, class or section
- [x] `E11-11` **Charts worth looking at.** Andy: *"The graph in growth is abysmal to say the least… These can be made so impressive."* The first attempt drew the data and nothing else — no y axis, no dates, no baseline, and `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, which stretches the drawing to its container and distorts every slope in it, throwing away the one thing a line chart communicates. `lib/admin/chart.ts` now gives both screens `columns()` and `area()` with a **rounded axis** (`niceScale` picks 1/2/5 × a power of ten so the top gridline is a number a person would choose, and always starts at zero — a truncated axis exaggerates every change on it), **thinned x labels** that always keep the last one, gridlines, a baseline, and a `<title>` on every mark, which is a native tooltip with no JavaScript and is what a screen reader announces. Still zero third-party assets: a chart library is impossible under `script-src 'self'` and unnecessary at twenty lines of geometry. 21 tests, including that the escaping holds when a school name contains markup
- [x] `E11-12` **A sales report, by the day the order was taken.** Andy: *"Currently orders are only seen for the day the delivery is requested for, I want to see sales (orders) placed for a given day. That way I can see when a large order day comes — I want to be able to see growth or reduction in orders."* `/admin/sales`, gated on `reports.view` **and** `orders.view_financials` — the same pair `/orders` uses, because growth percentages and average order value are our commercial view and do not belong in a school office's navigation. Day, week and month grains, plus by school. **The design decision that mattered was the timestamp.** `placed_at` is set at checkout and is populated on 100% of real orders; `confirmed_at` is null on every unpaid one, so bucketing by it would have silently dropped demand; `created_at` is a row-lifecycle field, not a business fact. **And the day must be IST**: of the first real production orders, **three of four were placed after 18:30 UTC** — parents order in the evening — so a UTC bucket would have put most orders on the wrong day, in the one report whose purpose is to say which day was busy. The query shifts both bounds by 5h30 and makes the upper one **exclusive**, or an order at midnight would count as the previous day's last. Every figure carries its change against the window of the same length immediately before, so a shorter month never reads as a decline, and a rise from zero is shown as "first in this period" rather than as an infinite percentage. **Kept as one row set:** `summarise` now keys by service day, placed day and school, so all six axes fold from the same rows and a test asserts their totals are identical — two separate queries would have let the sales total disagree with the revenue total. Rejected: folding this into `/reports` behind a basis toggle, because the two have different defaults (this range ends today, that one ends tomorrow), different comparisons and different audiences, and a number whose meaning depends on a control above it is how a report gets quoted wrongly
- [ ] `E11-13` **Lead time — how far ahead people order.** The natural next question once `E11-12` shows when orders arrive: the gap between `placed_at` and `service_date`, which says whether the cutoff is set in the right place and whether a menu published on Friday is being ordered from over the weekend. Both columns are already read
- [x] `E11-14` **Sales shows paid orders only, and schools by their code.** Three things Andy reported. **(1) "Date filter is not working."** It works — driving the live page with a real session, `2026-08-22`–`24` gives "No paid orders in this range" and `15`–`16` gives 3. It *looked* broken because all six production orders were placed inside one week, so every wide range returns the same set, and because the screen said "6 orders placed" beside "₹42.00 net" — one paid order's worth. That contradiction was the real complaint, and (3) removes it. The status line now echoes the active range so a change is visible even when the numbers are unchanged. **(2) School labels.** `groupRows` labelled school buckets with the full name, and "Amity International, Mohali" on a chart axis beside "Gem Public School" is unreadable at any width. The axis now shows `school.code` upper-cased — short, unique and already the identifier the importer matches on — while the table and every tooltip keep the full name. A refused school read falls back to the name rather than showing a bare uuid. **(3) Unpaid orders removed.** *"This report is 'Sales' — it does not make sense to show unpaid orders here."* Correct: `grossPaise` had always excluded them, but the **counts** did not, which is exactly why the screen contradicted itself. `Bucket` gains an explicit `paid` — stated rather than derived, because three screens were each computing `orders - cancelled - pending` and that subtraction becomes quietly wrong the day a status is added. Headline, charts and table are all paid-only; the Unpaid and Cancelled columns are gone. Also fixed: the table reversed its rows for "newest first", which on the school axis inverted the value ranking and put the smallest school above the largest, contradicting the chart beside it
- [x] `E11-15` **The adoption funnel, with the drop-off as a count.** Andy: *"Registered parents → parents who added at least one child → parents who placed a first order → parents who ordered again. Show the drop-off between each step as a number, not just a percentage. A parent who registered and never added a child is someone I can email."* Added to `/admin/growth` rather than a new screen — it is already the separate page `/admin/people` was told to stay out of. Every step counts **accounts**, carries the number who stopped there, and names **what to do about them**, because a step that states only a number is a step nobody acts on. **An unpaid order is not a conversion:** counting a parent who reached checkout and never paid would make the funnel flatter than the business is, which is the one direction it must not lie in. Plus parents active in the last 7 days, paid orders per day, and average order value over paid orders only. **The stuck list is the one place a personal detail appears** — an email, capped at 25, newest first, and its shape has nowhere to put anything about a child even by accident. The page intro used to promise "no parent is named", which stopped being true the moment that list existed, and now says exactly what is shown and why
